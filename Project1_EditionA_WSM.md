## Synthetic enterprise-to-equity bridge check

**Source: SYNTHETIC TEACHING CASE** — the Week 2 fallback. This is a mechanics check only and is not combined with WSM filing figures.

| Bridge item | Amount | Treatment |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Enterprise value | $1,000 million | Starting point |
| Add: non-operating cash | $100 million | Non-operating asset |
| Less: debt | ($250 million) | Debt claim |
| Less: noncontrolling interest | ($20 million) | Non-common claim |
| Less: unfunded pension | ($10 million) | Debt-like claim |
| = Common equity value | **$820 million** | $1,000 + $100 − $250 − $20 − $10 |
| ÷ diluted shares | 100 million | Share basis |
| = Value per diluted share | **$8.20 per share** | $820 million ÷ 100 million shares |

Reconciliation anchors: NOPAT = $112.5 million and FCFF = $77.5 million. A unit/sign failure that would reverse the interpretation is adding debt instead of subtracting it when converting enterprise value to common equity value.

## Consequential assumptions

- The synthetic bridge is a separate mechanics check; it does not use WSM cash, debt, or diluted shares.
- For later WSM valuation work, the August 2 cash balance and the reported absence of revolving-credit and letter-of-credit borrowings must be assessed under the course’s debt and excess-cash convention. Lease liabilities are excluded unless that convention capitalizes them.
- Q2 company comparable-brand revenue growth is informative for current demand, but it is not a full-year result.
- Diluted weighted-average shares are an acceptable initial denominator; a later valuation should reconcile them to current basic shares and incremental dilution at the valuation-date share price.

## Unknowns and research plan

1. Determine the market price and market capitalization on the selected valuation date, then calculate trading multiples and implied equity value.
2. Analyze the durability of comparable growth by brand, channel, and category, with particular attention to Pottery Barn, the largest brand.
3. Remove the net $0.74 Q2 tariff-refund-related EPS benefit and model continuing gross-margin exposure to tariffs, merchandise costs, shipping, and occupancy.
4. Assess whether the August 2 cash balance is excess after seasonal inventory, capital expenditure, repurchases, dividends, and working-capital needs.
5. Establish the appropriate treatment of operating leases and stock-based dilution under the course valuation convention.
6. Compare WSM’s margins, growth, capital returns, and valuation to relevant specialty-home and discretionary-retail peers.
